# MaydaLabs Logo Exploration

## Brand brief

MaydaLabs is a product and growth studio that turns ambiguous founder context into working software and repeatable growth systems. The identity should feel editorial, technical, independent, and sharp without looking like a generic SaaS, analytics, or AI company.

## Production requirements

- Recognizable at 16px and confident at large display sizes.
- Works as a one-color mark before any accent color is added.
- Supports a horizontal wordmark, square social avatar, favicon, and motion treatment.
- Uses the existing black, warm cream, and Bitcoin-orange palette.
- Avoids charts, growth bars, circuit-board cliches, literal laboratory glassware, and generic spark icons.

## Direction A: Signal Fold

A single continuous line folds into an abstract M. The open beginning represents ambiguity; the orange endpoint represents a resolved signal. This is the most minimal and flexible direction.

## Direction B: System Gate

A heavier architectural M built from two connected structures. It presents MaydaLabs as a place where ideas pass through a deliberate system and emerge as finished products. This is the most assertive and editorial direction.

## Direction C: Open Orbit

An open circular field surrounds a compact M path and ends in a signal point. It connects naturally to the orbital visual language already established in the website hero. This is the most ownable motion-system direction.

## Recommendation

Take Direction A or C forward. Direction A is the strongest pure logo; Direction C creates the richest website and social motion language. Direction B is useful if the brand should feel more architectural and less technology-forward.

## Selected direction: Signal Gate

The final working direction develops the open-gate concept from the external ChatGPT exploration rather than selecting one of the first three monograms. Two continuous structural sides frame one orange decision point. The mark stays legible at favicon size, while its motion language can transform scattered noise into the central signal before the gates settle into place.
