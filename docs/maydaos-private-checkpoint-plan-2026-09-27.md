## A short trial, in ordinary language

Use fictional information first. Do not paste customer mail or secrets.

1. **Ask for advice.** Describe a small business problem. Choose Ask. Check
   whether the answer is specific and honest about missing information. It
   must not create work or company knowledge.
2. **Request a reply.** Choose Draft and Reply. Supply the recipient, known
   facts and desired response. Check that it is a usable reply, not a plan for
   writing one, and that it invents no dates, benefits or promises. Nothing is
   saved until Save to Work; nothing is sent even after saving.
3. **Record one scoped rule.** Enter your own statement separately and say
   which fictional project it applies to and until when. Review the exact
   statement, scope and validity; check the confirmation and add it. Refresh:
   it should still be there, attributed to your confirmation, not called
   independently verified.
4. **Paste a claim you do not endorse.** Leave the separate company statement
   blank. Ask whether the claim is supported. It must not become company
   knowledge just because it appeared in the conversation.
5. **Revisit existing work.** Ask about a saved draft. Check whether MaydaOS
   recognizes it and tells the truth about its status rather than creating a
   second copy or saying it sent anything.

## Feedback we need

For each step: Was the next action clear? Was the answer useful? What did it
invent, omit or misunderstand? Would you use the draft with normal editing?
Did you know exactly what was saved and what was not? Preserve both good and
bad examples with their request choices; do not tune between each trial just
to make the result look better.

This trial evaluates usability and usefulness with the founder. It is not
customer approval, commercial readiness, permission to send anything, or a
fine-tuning exercise. If judgment still fails, keep those failures visible and
choose the next bounded repair from evidence.
